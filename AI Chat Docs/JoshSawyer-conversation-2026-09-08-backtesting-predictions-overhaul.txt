[PART B -- backtesting all prediction algorithms, LaTeX report]

[USER]
[Asked for all prediction algorithms in the app to be backtested, and a
LaTeX-compiled PDF report produced on their current accuracy, where they
could realistically improve, and how accurate they actually are.]

[ASSISTANT]
Backtested the Elo win-probability model and the Four Factors margin
model using a chronological train/validation split (never a random
split, since predicting the past from the future is the classic leakage
mistake for time-series sports data) plus permutation testing to
separate real signal from noise. Compiled results into a LaTeX report
via tectonic, producing docs/reports/prediction-accuracy-report.pdf
(v1).

Over several follow-up rounds ("what's next, is there room for
improvement or do we leave it like this?", "lets revisit the
season-boundary reset question"), iterated the models with the same
discipline every time -- propose a change, backtest it properly on a
held-out split, only ship if it actually validated:

  - Re-tuned Elo's K-factor and related constants against a larger
    dataset once more historical seasons were pulled in (see Part D).
  - Added a season-boundary Elo rating reset (partial regression toward
    a neutral baseline at the start of each new season, rather than
    carrying full ratings across an offseason unchanged) -- validated
    this genuinely improved calibration before shipping it.
  - Tried an opponent-strength adjustment to the fantasy-points
    predictor -- backtested worse than baseline on a validation split,
    REJECTED, documented as a rejected idea rather than silently dropped.
  - Tried a multiply-based minutes-times-points-per-minute model for
    fantasy points -- an initial backtest looked like a clean win, but
    the assistant caught its own bug: the backtest script's DNP
    (zero-minutes game) handling didn't match the actual production
    logic being validated. Corrected the script to match production
    exactly, and the apparent edge vanished entirely. REJECTED. Documented
    as a specific lesson: don't trust a backtest number until it's
    checked against the *exact* logic being validated, not an
    approximation of it.
  - Shipped a minutes-trend adjustment (nudging a fantasy-points
    prediction based on whether a player's recent minutes deviate from
    their longer-run baseline) after it validated as a real, consistent
    improvement across two different validation splits.

Found and fixed a real, unrelated data-correctness bug while scoping the
multi-season data pull: PlayerGameStat rows were being attributed to a
player's CURRENT team rather than the team they actually played for in
that specific historical game, corrupting stats for any traded player's
past games. Fixed at the ingestion layer.

Iterated the LaTeX report through v2-v5 as the underlying models changed,
each version documenting both what shipped and what was tried and
rejected, with the actual validation numbers behind each decision, not
just the final state.

This work is verified against commit af0f185 / 6b25aa9 ("feat: multi-
season prediction model improvements and backtesting"), which touched:
apps/ingestion/games.py, ingest.py, ingest_historical_season.py (new);
apps/optimizer/predict.py, test_predict.py (new); apps/predictor/elo.py,
